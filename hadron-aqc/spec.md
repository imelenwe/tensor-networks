# Hadron AQC — spec

Applying AQCtensor's Algorithm 1 (same method as `AQC/aqctensor-algorithm1.ipynb`) to the SU(2) Loop-String-Hadron (LSH) circuit from IBM's tutorial — compress its deep Trotter circuit into a shallow trained AQC circuit that reproduces the same physics with far fewer two-qubit gates.

## Why this matters (not just "does it run")

The tutorial's own approach doesn't compress anything — it submits the full deep circuit (>3,400 two-qubit gates at 60 qubits) directly to real hardware and fights the resulting noise with mitigation tricks (TREX, Pauli twirling, 10,000 shots) plus a classical cross-check (Pauli Propagation). That's a brute-force ceiling: more lattice sites or more simulated time means more gates, and eventually noise wins regardless of mitigation. AQC's payoff is the same physics with a fraction of the two-qubit gates — either a cleaner result at the same scale, or room to push past where brute force stops working. It's also a real validation of whether the compression technique (proven on the toy XYZ chain) generalizes to a real, actively-studied problem.

## The pieces, in plain terms

- **`construct_circuit(...)`** — the Trotter target circuit (this project's equivalent of `second_order_trotter_circuit`). Per step: SWAP → Pair-gate layer → SWAP → Pair-gate layer → SWAP → Electric-gate layer → Mass-term `Rz`. Trusted as-is (published, tested code) — not re-verifying its internals, same way we don't re-verify Qiskit's own gates.
- **Two initial states**: vacuum (SCV, empty lattice) and meson (vacuum + one excitation at the center). Params used in the tutorial: `num_lattice_point=6` (12 qubits, small/simulator scale) and `num_lattice_point=30` (60 qubits, hardware scale); `c=0.15, theta=0.01, m=0.03`.
- **The observable**: per-qubit Z-measurement → `get_probabilities` → `get_number` turns that into the staggered fermion number `n_f(r)` at each site → `calculate_difference` subtracts vacuum from meson to isolate the real signal. This is the physical quantity any AQC-compressed circuit needs to reproduce, not just global fidelity.

## Steps

0. [ ] Fix the MPS core before reusing it — `apply_2q_gate_mps_tensor` truncates without canonical form (the cut isn't the best one) and never renormalizes (norm leaks: a 12-qubit random circuit at χ=16 ends at norm² 0.88, reported fidelity 0.78 vs true 0.89). Harmless for the XYZ run (fidelity measured identical across χ=32/64/128 — confirmed unaffected), but this circuit needs χ in the hundreds, so nearly every gate will cut. Fix: move the orthogonality centre onto each gate's pair with a QR sweep before the SVD, renormalize `S` after the cut, assert the two tensors are neighbours. Verify: norm stays 1 under heavy truncation; truncated fidelity matches statevector on a small random circuit; XYZ `L=50` result unchanged. Also add a `⟨Z_q⟩`-from-MPS function here (needed for `get_number` later) — watch for the same kind of index-reversal subtlety as `tensor_idx=L-1-q`, plus the tutorial's own `evs[::-1]` reversal.
1. [ ] Port `pair_hamiltonian_circuit`, `electric_hamiltonian_circuit`, `construct_circuit`, `get_probabilities`, `get_number`, `calculate_difference` into a new notebook here, verbatim. Ground truth at 12 qubits: `Statevector(construct_circuit(...))` directly — **not** an independently-derived exact Hamiltonian (the tutorial's own gate-parameter formulas are internally inconsistent if read literally: `c=δτ·x=0.15>0` implies `θ<0` by their formula, but the tutorial uses `θ=+0.01`), so this correctly measures compression error only, not also Trotter error.
2. [ ] Establish baselines before training anything. Qiskit's own `transpile(optimization_level=3)` CX count, transpiled **with the tutorial's actual linear-chain coupling map** (a naive coupling-map-free transpile lets the optimizer "delete" SWAPs by relabeling qubits — not achievable on real hardware, discard any number computed that way) — **measured: 3455 at 60 qubits/10 steps, 611 at 12 qubits/10 steps — this is the real bar AQC needs to beat**, not the O1-transpiled 4615/811 the tutorial itself used. (3455 lines up with the tutorial's own stated ">3400 two-qubit gates" — good cross-check.) Also run the tutorial's own Pauli Propagation code as an independent large-scale `n_f(r,t)` reference.
3. [ ] χ-convergence study. Entanglement grows faster here than the XYZ chain — measured: χ=48 at 2 Trotter steps, χ=176 at 5 steps, still growing after 10 (consistent with quimb needing χ≈800). Test at an intermediate scale (20-24 qubits, where exact statevector is still feasible) so truncation error is checked directly, not assumed.
4. [ ] Build the AQC ansatz — reuse the brickwork/triplet-block structure for the Pair/Electric gate roles plus the mass-term rotation. Expressibility already confirmed: triplet block (preceded by `Rz⊗Rz`) fits identity/Pair/Electric/SWAP/SWAP·Pair to <1e-10 infidelity, so a **numeric-fit** Trotter-init (not hand-derived trig formulas) is viable. Trotter-init must seed from a **coarser** Trotter approximation than the target: each Trotter step has 5 two-qubit layers (Pair, SWAP, Pair, SWAP, Electric), each brickwork layer ≈90 two-qubit gates at 60 qubits, so a same-depth init reproduces ~4400 gates — loses to the O3 baseline (3455) from Step 2. To beat it, the ansatz needs to stay under ~38 layers, i.e. roughly ≤7 coarse Trotter steps (`c, theta, m` scaled up to cover the same total time). Worth testing: the Pair gate conserves particle number but the generic triplet ansatz doesn't — a number-conserving ansatz variant may explain Mriganka's reported edge.
5. [ ] Training — **decide the gradient method before writing this**: finite differences (as used in the XYZ notebook) won't scale to this problem's estimated parameter count (7,000-9,000) and required χ — needs analytic gradients or autodiff (JAX/PyTorch), plus optimizer checkpointing (long runs must survive interruption). Also worth clarifying with Prof Indrakshi whether "the original AQC" she's comparing against is this from-scratch pipeline or IBM's own `qiskit-addon-aqc-tensor` package — changes whether this step builds from scratch or wraps that package.
6. [ ] Train and verify at small scale (12 qubits) — global fidelity **and** the local `n_f(r)` profile (per Emil M's point). Measure error specifically on `N_diff = |n_f(meson) - n_f(vacuum)|`, not just per-circuit `n_f` — it's a difference of two large numbers, which amplifies relative error.
7. [ ] Scale up — 20-24 qubits, then 60 (matching the tutorial's hardware example). Keep `num_lattice_point` even (layout only confirmed correct for even site counts). **Full deliverable is 20 circuits** — one per Trotter-step count `t=1..10`, × {meson, vacuum} — not just the `t=10` endpoint; the tutorial's own heatmaps plot the whole range. If appending Trotter steps rather than retraining per time point: `construct_circuit`'s step-0 skips its first SWAP layer, so appended steps must start from step ≥1 — verify at 12 qubits first. Still undecided: train separately per Trotter-step count vs. train once and append (per [[project_aqc_algorithm1_complete]]'s Step 14 pattern).
8. [ ] Compare against Mriganka's results — needs an explicit fixed protocol (same circuit/time/qubit-count/CX-budget/metrics) to be a fair comparison, not just two different numbers reported independently.

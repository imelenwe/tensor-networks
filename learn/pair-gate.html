The Pair Gate: how a particle hops

Brick 1 of the brickwork circuit, worked out by hand on two qubits. States are written |q0 q1⟩.

← Back to the learn index

1. The gate

From brickwork_annotated.png. The dashed X on q1 is the vacuum's starting flip; the orange box is the pair gate.

In order: CX(control q1 → q0), H(q1), Rz(−0.15)(q1), CX(q0 → q1), Rz(+0.15)(q1), CX(q0 → q1), H(q1), CX(q1 → q0). 4 CNOTs. The 0.15 is the kinetic strength.

The only rule needed for the Rz turns: Rz(θ)|0⟩ = e−iθ/2|0⟩, Rz(θ)|1⟩ = e+iθ/2|1⟩ — it only adds a phase, never changes probabilities. (As an operator: Rz(θ) = cos(θ/2)·I − i·sin(θ/2)·Z.)

Why the two Rz turns don't cancel. Rz(θ) then Rz(−θ) on the same qubit would cancel — but a CNOT sits between them and flips q1 (because q0 = 1). The term that got e−iθ/2 as "q1 = 0" is "q1 = 1" when the second Rz arrives, so it gets e−iθ/2 again: the phases add. Had q0 been 0, they would cancel. An effect on q1 that depends on q0 is exactly a two-qubit interaction.

3. |ψ8⟩ is two Bell states with opposite phases

With the Bell states β01 = (|01⟩ + |10⟩)/√2 and β11 = (|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2:

|ψ8⟩ = (1/√2) [ e^(−iθ) β01  +  e^(+iθ) β11 ]

start: |01⟩ = (1/√2) [ β01 + β11 ]

Same two Bell states, same weights. The whole pair gate did only one thing: it turned β01's phase by e−iθ and β11's by e+iθ.

Why these two Bell states? They are the eigenstates of the hopping operator XX + YY. Using X|0⟩=|1⟩, X|1⟩=|0⟩, Y|0⟩=i|1⟩, Y|1⟩=−i|0⟩:

(XX + YY)|01⟩ = |10⟩ + |10⟩ = 2|10⟩
(XX + YY)|10⟩ = 2|01⟩
(XX + YY)|00⟩ = |11⟩ − |11⟩ = 0      (same for |11⟩)

so  (XX + YY) β01 = +2 β01      (XX + YY) β11 = −2 β11

An exponential of an operator turns each eigenstate by its own phase. With eigenvalues +2 and −2:

pair gate = exp(−iθ (XX + YY) / 2),   θ = −0.15
β01 → e^(−iθ) β01      β11 → e^(+iθ) β11      |00⟩, |11⟩ unchanged

Checked by computer: the 8-gate circuit equals this exponential exactly, for every input.

4. The hop

Collect the terms of |ψ8⟩ by basis state instead:

  • |10⟩: (e−iθ − eiθ)/2 = −i sin θ
  • |01⟩: (e−iθ + eiθ)/2 = cos θ
|ψ8⟩ = cos θ |01⟩ − i sin θ |10⟩

With θ = −0.15:

outcomeamplitudeprobability
|01⟩ (where it started)cos 0.15 = 0.98997.8%
|10⟩ (the 1 moved to q0)+i·sin 0.15 = 0.149i2.2%
This is the hop. The "1" (a particle) moves from q1 to q0 with a 2.2% chance per Trotter step. The total number of 1s stays exactly one — hopping moves particles, it doesn't create or destroy them here. Over many steps these small amounts build up, and that is the dynamics we are simulating. And the two Bell states getting opposite phases is why the hop happens.

Key points

  • Pair gate = 8 gates, 4 CNOTs, = exp(−iθ(XX+YY)/2) with θ = −0.15 (kinetic strength 0.15).
  • Rz only adds phases; the CNOT between the two Rz turns makes them add instead of cancel.
  • β01 and β11 are the eigenstates of XX + YY (eigenvalues +2, −2); the gate gives them opposite phases.
  • Result: |01⟩ → cos θ |01⟩ − i sin θ |10⟩: the particle hops with probability sin²θ ≈ 2.2% per step; the number of particles is conserved.

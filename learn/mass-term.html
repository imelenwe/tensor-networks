The Mass Term: one-qubit phases

The dashed Rz turns at the end of each Trotter step.

1. The gate

The dashed Rz boxes at the end of the step are the mass term: one-qubit turns, no CNOTs.

Each qubit gets Rz(±0.03) (mass 0.03). Like the electric gate it only adds phases: Rz(m)|0⟩ = e−im/2|0⟩, Rz(m)|1⟩ = e+im/2|1⟩.

2. The sign pattern: per qubit or per site

q0q1q2q3q4q5…
earlier circuit (this picture)−+−+−+per qubit
newer circuit files−−++−−per site

In code: q % 2 (earlier) vs (q // 2) % 2 (newer). The physics wants both qubits of a site to get the same sign, alternating from site to site (quarks on even sites, antiquarks on odd sites).

3. What it means

  • Mass = energy cost of a particle on a site. Configurations with more particles turn their phase differently, so at the next pair gates, hops that create particles add up less: pair creation is suppressed.
  • Why the sign pattern matters: with the per-qubit pattern the mass phase did not depend on where the particles are (as the group pointed out), so it had no effect — we saw mass 0, 0.03 and 0.3 give nearly identical results. With the per-site pattern the phase depends on the configuration, and the mass changes the dynamics.
Same lesson as the electric gate: a phase-only gate does nothing on its own, but sets the energy of each configuration — and that decides which hops add up over many steps.

The Mass Term: why the old signs did nothing

The small Rz turns at the end of each Trotter step, read off the old diagram, then worked on the same 4-qubit state as the earlier pages. States are written |q0 q1 q2 q3⟩.

1. Where the mass term is (old circuit)

Old circuit (brickwork_annotated_old.png). Outlined in red: the 12 mass Rz gates, one per qubit — easy to miss because each pair sits right after its electric gate.

Every qubit gets one Rz(±0.03) (mass 0.03). They come in the same pairs as the electric gates — each pair is one site: (q0,q1), (q2,q3), … (q10,q11). Like the electric gate, they only add phases: Rz(+m) gives a 1 the phase e+im/2 and a 0 the phase e−im/2; Rz(−m) the opposite.

Corrected circuit (brickwork_annotated.png, drawn by draw_brickwork.py): q0, q1 −0.03; q2, q3 +0.03; q4, q5 −0.03; … Everything else is unchanged.

Old: the sign flips every qubit — inside every site the two qubits get opposite signs, and every site looks the same. Correct: both qubits of a site share a sign, and the sign flips from site to site. In code: q % 2 → (q // 2) % 2. To fix the diagram, flip the signs on q1, q2, q5, q6, q9, q10.

3. Worked on our 4-qubit state

The state after the electric gate (electric phases left out — they don't affect this point). (q0,q1) is one site, (q2,q3) the next:

cos²θ |0011⟩ − i sinθ cosθ |0110⟩ − i sinθ cosθ |1001⟩ − sin²θ |1100⟩

The rule for one qubit. Rz never changes a bit, it only multiplies by a phase — and the phase depends on the bit and on the sign of the turn:

Rz(+m) |0⟩ = e^(−im/2) |0⟩        Rz(+m) |1⟩ = e^(+im/2) |1⟩
Rz(−m) |0⟩ = e^(+im/2) |0⟩        Rz(−m) |1⟩ = e^(−im/2) |1⟩

The rule for a whole term. Each qubit gets its own Rz, so a term's phase is the product of its four qubits' phases. Example, old signs (−, +, −, +) on |0011⟩:

q0 = 0 under Rz(−m) → e^(+im/2)
q1 = 0 under Rz(+m) → e^(−im/2)
q2 = 1 under Rz(−m) → e^(−im/2)
q3 = 1 under Rz(+m) → e^(+im/2)
product                → e^(0) = 1

Same rule, new signs (−, −, +, +) on |0011⟩: e+im/2 · e+im/2 · e+im/2 · e+im/2 = e+2im.

Doing this for all four terms:

termwhere the 1s areold signs (−, +, −, +)new signs (−, −, +, +)
|0011⟩both on site (q2 q3)1e+2im
|0110⟩one on each site11
|1001⟩one on each site11
|1100⟩both on site (q0 q1)1e−2im

Checked by computer.

  • Old signs: every term gets exactly the same phase. Inside each site the two Rz turns are opposite, so they cancel for every term — the mass term does nothing at all. That is why changing the mass (0, 0.03, 0.3) changed nothing in our old results.
  • New signs: the phase depends on which site the 1s sit on. 1s on the "−" site get one phase, 1s on the "+" site the opposite, one on each and they cancel. The terms now carry different phases.
Why that matters. At the next Trotter step the pair gate mixes these terms — that is the hop. Terms with different phases no longer add up in step, so how the 1s move from step to step changes. In plain words: with the corrected signs, a 1 sitting on a "−" site behaves differently from a 1 on a "+" site — being on one kind of site costs a different energy, and that steers where the particles go. That is what "the mass term now matters" means — and why the old setup's mass term was useless.

Key points

  • Mass term = one Rz(±0.03) per qubit at the end of each step, in site pairs; phase only, no CNOTs.
  • Old circuit: signs alternate per qubit → opposite inside each site → every term gets the same phase → no effect.
  • Corrected: same sign inside a site, alternating site to site → phase depends on where the particles are → changes the hopping at the next step.
